CH-3 — China Three-Factor Model

Replication of the three-factor model from Liu, Stambaugh and Yuan's Size and Value in China: a market factor (MKT), a size factor (SMB), and a value factor VMG (value-minus-growth) built on the earnings-to-price ratio — the paper's key finding being that EP beats book-to-market as the value signal in the A-share market. Portfolios are formed on the last trading day of each month using 2×3 double sorts on size (median split) and EP (30/40/30), value-weighted within each cell. The live model holds SMB and VMG in equal weight as a long-short portfolio, matching the backtest strategy Factor_CH3_Equal_SMB_VMG. The investable sample excludes the bottom 30% of stocks by market cap (the paper's shell-value filter).

CH-4 — Three Factors plus Turnover Sentiment

CH-4 extends CH-3 with a sentiment factor PMO (pessimistic-minus-optimistic) designed for the retail-dominated A-share market. Stocks are ranked each month on abnormal turnover — the 21-day average turnover divided by the 252-day average. Low-abnormal-turnover ("pessimistic") stocks are bought and high-abnormal-turnover ("optimistic") stocks are shorted, again through a size-neutral 2×3 sort. The live model holds SMB, VMG and PMO in equal weight, matching the backtest strategy Factor_CH4_Equal_SMB_VMG_PMO.

Eight-Factor — Extended Multi-Factor Model

An extended framework layering momentum, liquidity and default risk on top of a Fama-French-five-style core, with value measured by EP to stay consistent with the China evidence: MKT, SMB (FF-2015 style — the average of six size spreads, one per characteristic sort), HML_EP (value), RMW (profitability, ROE), CMA (investment, low-minus-high YoY asset growth), MOM (12-month momentum excluding the latest month), LIQ (low-minus-high turnover) and DR (default risk, high-minus-low leverage). A COMPOSITE factor z-scores the six characteristics into one signal. The live model trades the COMPOSITE long-short; individual factors are tracked alongside.

Factor Risk Evolution — rolling CAPM

Each factor run through a 36-month rolling CAPM (factor = α + β·MKT + ε) across 2000–2025, to see whether its alpha is turning into a market-risk premium. How to read it: if α trends down toward zero while |β| trends up, the factor's return is converting from alpha into compensation for market risk. If α fades but β stays near zero, the alpha is simply decaying (arbitraged away or regime change), not becoming market risk. Rising volatility / drawdown with a falling Sharpe signals the premium is getting riskier (crowding). Because these are market-neutral long-short factors, β is small by construction — the dashed line adds each extra factor's rolling alpha versus CH-4, a more sensitive check of whether it is being explained away by the other factors.

Long-Only Live Strategies

Investable long-only portfolios tracked live, complementing the long-short factor models. EP Top 30% buys stocks in the top three EP deciles; MultiFactor Top 20% ranks stocks on a composite of EP, small size and low abnormal turnover and buys the top quintile — each in an equal-weighted (EW) and value-weighted (VW) version. LIQ Long-Only (VW) holds only the long leg of the liquidity factor — the lowest-turnover (least liquid) stocks, size-neutral and value-weighted — to capture the illiquidity premium without shorting. All rebalanced monthly with 20 bps one-way costs.
